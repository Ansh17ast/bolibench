CORE RESEARCH THESIS

The Indian Voice AI Evaluation Paradox

Standard speech recognition benchmarks evaluate models using aggregate Word Error Rate (WER). An ASR engine achieving an 8% WER on an Indian conversational benchmark is typically considered state-of-the-art. Yet in a voice agent deployment, that single 8% error is often catastrophic.

❌

Aggregate WER Blindspot

If an ASR model confuses "teen packet" (three packets) with "do packet" (two packets), it achieves 33% WER on the phrase. But downstream in the warehouse management system, this causes an incorrect physical dispatch, financial reconciliation failure, and customer refund dispute.

⚡

BoliBench Paired-Contrast Testing

BoliBench tests voice AI agents against minimal semantic contrast pairs. By testing how the speech engine handles minimal token shifts in critical slots (quantities, negations, financial amounts), BoliBench isolates whether the downstream voice agent's decision collapses.

Evaluation Methodology & Taxonomy

1. Paired-Contrast Design

Each benchmark item consists of Utterance A and Utterance B differing by a single high-impact variable:

  • Quantities & Numbers: Minimal numeral contrasts (e.g. "Do packet" vs "Teen packet", "Pachees" vs "Pachaas").
  • Negation & Reversal: Subtle affirmation vs cancellation (e.g. "Haan confirm kardo" vs "Nahi cancel kardo").
  • Code-Switching (Hinglish/Tanglish): Alternational and insertional language mixing.
  • Currency & Denominations: Units and multipliers (e.g. "Pachas hazar" vs "Pachas sau").
  • Discourse Particles: Particles like "toh", "hi", "na" that flip conversational intent.
  • Cross-Lingual Homophones: Words phonetically similar across English and Indian languages.

2. Deterministic Scoring

Unlike slow, non-deterministic LLM judges, BoliBench utilizes a deterministic evaluation pipeline:

  • Normalizer: Normalizes whitespace and common Indian script variations while preserving critical distinction tokens.
  • Slot Extractor: Extracts critical entity slots (quantities, negation states, amounts, products).
  • Action Comparator: Maps extracted slots to agent action signatures. If both contrast utterances produce identical actions, a Semantic Distinction Collapse is recorded.
  • Failure Taxonomy: Classifies failure modes into quantity_error, missed_negation, entity_substitution, command_intent_error, or provider_error.

Linguistic Realities & Limitations

01

Transliteration & Cross-Script WER Elevation

In Indian speech-to-text, providers frequently output either Latin script (e.g. "Do packet chahiye") or native Devanagari (e.g. "दो पैकेट चाहिए"). Standard WER algorithms compute string edit distance between Latin references and Devanagari hypotheses, creating an artificial 100% WER score even when the transcript is acoustically perfect. BoliBench clearly displays the raw transcript without silent rewriting and evaluates semantic field extraction independently of script representation.

02

Deterministic Mocks vs Real Acoustic Recordings

Text simulations provide reproducible, zero-cost CI/CD regression testing. However, synthetic text contrasts do not simulate acoustic channel noise, microphone clipping, regional accents, or conversational speech overlap. BoliBench enforces strict separation: synthetic simulations are never labeled as observed provider errors.

03

Live Provider Duration & Safety Enforcement

To protect competition programme credits, live paired evaluations strictly require WAV audio under 60 seconds with server-side duration parsing, single-use 5-minute cryptographic authorization tokens, and same-origin CSRF verification. When the upstream provider encounters an operational outage (HTTP 502/504), BoliBench audits the failure neutrally without labeling it as a semantic model failure.
